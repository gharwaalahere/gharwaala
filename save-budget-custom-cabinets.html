5 Ways to Save Money on Custom Kitchen Cabinets

Cabinets are the biggest single cost in a modular kitchen. These five decisions can trim the bill noticeably, without leaving you with a kitchen that sags, swells or squeaks in three years.

By Gharwaala Design Team | Published March 14, 2026 | Updated October 6, 2026 | 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Use BWP plywood only where water is a real risk; HDHMR is fine for overhead cabinets.
  • Standard module sizes are cheaper and faster to produce than fully bespoke dimensions.
  • Spend on the hardware you touch daily (hinges, drawer channels); skip gadgets you won't use.
  • High-gloss laminate can deliver an acrylic-like look for less.
  • Freeze your design and appliance list before production. Changes later are expensive.

Cutting costs on a kitchen is easy. Cutting costs without regretting it is the hard part. The cheapest quote often hides thinner boards, unbranded channels and edge banding that peels within a monsoon or two.

The trick is knowing which parts of a cabinet carry the load and which are mostly cosmetic. Here are five places where homeowners can save, and one list of things you should never cut.

1. Put premium materials only where they're needed

Not every cabinet faces the same conditions. The sink base deals with leaks and daily splashing; a wall cabinet above the counter almost never gets wet.

  • BWP plywood for the sink unit and other base cabinets near water.
  • HDHMR or good-quality commercial ply for overhead and tall units in dry zones.

This zoned approach keeps strength where it counts while reducing the overall board cost. Our Delhi NCR cost guide explains how material choices affect the final quote.

2. Design around standard module sizes

Modular kitchens are made from standard-width units. Odd widths and one-off dimensions take extra cutting, handling and wastage, which shows up in the price.

Ask your designer to plan the run in standard modules and use small fillers or open niches to absorb leftover gaps. You'll usually get the same storage for less, and a faster production time.

Easy win: replace one or two overhead cabinets with open shelves for everyday jars and cups. Fewer shutters means less hardware and lower cost. Just keep them away from the hob, where grease builds up.

3. Prioritise hardware you use every day

Hardware is where quotes balloon. Corner carousels, magic corners, motorised lift-ups and pull-out larders all add up quickly, and some end up barely used.

Worth paying for: branded soft-close hinges and quality drawer channels. You'll open them thousands of times a year.

Think twice about: elaborate corner mechanisms (a simple blind corner with a deep shelf may do), multiple specialised pull-outs, and accessories duplicated across several units.

4. Choose finishes strategically

Acrylic and PU look stunning, but you don't need them everywhere. Options to get a premium look for less:

  • High-gloss laminate instead of acrylic: a similar shine at a lower price.
  • Premium finish only on visible surfaces, such as overheads or an island, with practical matte laminate on base units.
  • Laminate interiors in a simple light colour. Nobody sees them, and light interiors make things easier to find.

Compare finishes in detail in our kitchen laminates guide.

5. Lock the design before production starts

The most expensive change is the one made after cutting begins. A new chimney model, a bigger fridge or a moved sink can mean scrapping shutters, re-drilling and re-ordering.

  1. Finalise appliance models and their exact dimensions first.
  2. Approve a 3D design and check every drawer and door opening.
  3. Confirm plumbing and electrical points match the drawing before installation day.

What you should never cut

Save on looks if you must. Never save on the parts that hold the kitchen together.
  • The sink-base board. Water damage here ruins the cabinet.
  • Edge banding quality. Poor edging lets moisture into the board and causes swelling.
  • Hinges and drawer channels. Cheap ones sag, squeak and fail first.
  • Proper installation and levelling. Even the best cabinets misalign on an unlevel platform.

Bonus: read quotes properly

Two quotes can look very different simply because they're pricing different things. Ask every vendor for a line-item breakdown naming board type, laminate brand and hardware brand. That's the only way to see whether a lower price is real savings or just a lower specification.

Frequently asked questions

How can I reduce the cost of a modular kitchen?

Use BWP plywood only in wet zones and HDHMR elsewhere, design around standard module sizes, prioritise everyday hardware over specialised gadgets, use high-gloss laminate instead of acrylic, and finalise appliances before production to avoid costly changes.

Is HDHMR good for kitchen cabinets?

HDHMR is a good, cost-effective choice for overhead and dry-zone cabinets. For the sink base and areas exposed to regular water, BWP plywood is the safer choice.

Which kitchen accessories are worth the money?

Branded soft-close hinges and good-quality drawer channels give the best value because they are used constantly. Deep drawers for utensils are usually more useful than complex corner mechanisms.

Are open shelves cheaper than kitchen cabinets?

Yes. Open shelves need no shutters, hinges or handles, so replacing a few wall cabinets with shelves can lower costs. Keep them away from the hob to avoid grease build-up.
