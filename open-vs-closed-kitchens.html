Open vs Closed Kitchens: The Verdict

By Gharwaala Design Team • February 18, 2026 • 7 min read

Are open kitchens practical in India? We explore the pros and cons of spatial layouts and ventilation systems to help you decide which layout suits your lifestyle.

The Open Kitchen Advantage

Open kitchens dissolve the boundary between cooking and living areas. They are perfect for entertaining, allowing the cook to interact with guests or keep an eye on children. They also make the entire home feel larger and more connected.

The Closed Kitchen Reality

Indian cooking often involves heavy spices, frying, and strong aromas. A closed kitchen contains the smoke, smells, and noise, preventing them from permeating the living room. It also hides the inevitable clutter of meal prep from guests.

"An open kitchen looks beautiful in magazines, but requires strict daily discipline to keep clean. A closed kitchen gives you the freedom to be messy while entertaining."

The Best of Both Worlds: The Semi-Open Kitchen

Many homeowners are opting for a hybrid approach. Installing sliding glass partitions or a large pass-through window offers the visual connectivity of an open layout with the functional isolation of a closed room.

Ventilation is Key

If you choose an open kitchen, investing in a high-suction, low-noise chimney (minimum 1200 m³/hr) is non-negotiable. Proper cross-ventilation will ensure your living room stays fresh.
