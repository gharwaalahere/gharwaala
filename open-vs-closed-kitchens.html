Open vs Closed Kitchen: Which Is Better for Indian Homes?

Open kitchens dominate design magazines. Indian cooking, with its tadka, frying and lingering masala, makes the decision more complicated. Here's how to choose.

By Gharwaala Design Team | Published February 18, 2026 | Updated October 6, 2026 | 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Open kitchens feel larger and more social but need strong ventilation for Indian cooking.
  • Closed kitchens contain smoke, smells, noise and clutter, which suits daily heavy cooking.
  • Semi-open layouts with glass partitions or sliding doors are a popular middle ground.
  • For open kitchens, a high-suction chimney (around 1200 m³/hr or more) is essential.
  • Choose by cooking style first, then by space and how you entertain.

The open kitchen is one of the most requested features in new Indian homes, and one of the most argued about at the dinner table. One family member pictures chatting with guests while cooking; another pictures the sofa smelling of last night's tadka.

Both are right. The best layout depends on how your household cooks, how much space you have and how you use the rest of your home. Here's a clear-eyed look at each option.

The case for open kitchens

  • More space and light. Removing a wall makes compact apartments feel larger and lets daylight reach the kitchen.
  • More social. You can talk to family, watch children or host guests while cooking.
  • Flexible layouts. Islands and breakfast counters double as dining, homework or work-from-home spots.
  • Modern feel. It suits contemporary apartments and is what many buyers look for.

The case for closed kitchens

Indian cooking often involves heavy spices, deep frying and tadkas, with aromas that linger for hours. A closed kitchen keeps smoke, grease, smells and noise from reaching your living room furniture, curtains and cushions. It also hides the clutter of active meal prep from guests, which is a real practical advantage.

  • Contains smoke and smells. Grease doesn't settle on living-room fabrics.
  • Hides the mess. No need to tidy before guests see the kitchen.
  • Quieter home. Pressure cooker whistles, mixer-grinders and exhaust noise stay contained.
  • More wall storage. Extra walls mean more room for cabinets and tall units.

The middle ground: semi-open kitchens

Many families now choose a semi-open layout, which keeps the openness while controlling smoke and smells:

  • Glass partitions: visual connection and light, with smells contained.
  • Sliding or folding doors: open when entertaining, closed during heavy cooking.
  • Breakfast counter or half-wall: defines the kitchen while keeping sightlines.
  • Wet and dry kitchen split: an open "show" kitchen for light prep, with a closed or utility kitchen for heavy cooking. This is common in larger homes and villas.

Ventilation: non-negotiable for open kitchens

If you choose an open kitchen, a high-suction, low-noise chimney (at least around 1200 m³/hr suction) is essential. Auto-clean and filterless chimneys reduce maintenance. Add cross-ventilation and a well-placed exhaust fan, and the living room stays fresh even on heavy cooking days.

Planning tip: plan the chimney duct route at the design stage. A short, straight duct with few bends works far better than a long, twisting one, whatever the suction rating says.

What about apartments in Delhi NCR?

In many builder apartments across Delhi NCR the kitchen position is fixed and walls may be structural, so check with your society or builder before removing any wall. Where a full open plan isn't possible, a widened opening, a pass-through counter or a glass partition can deliver much of the same feel. Explore options for your city: Delhi, Gurugram, Noida.

How to decide: a quick checklist

  1. How often do you deep-fry or do heavy tadka? Daily points towards closed or semi-open.
  2. Do you entertain often? Frequently points towards open or semi-open.
  3. Can you install a powerful chimney with a good duct route? If not, avoid fully open.
  4. Do you need maximum storage? Closed kitchens offer more wall space.
  5. Are there young children to keep an eye on? Open layouts help supervision.

The verdict

The right choice depends on your family's cooking habits. If you cook light meals and entertain often, go open, with serious ventilation. If your family does heavy Indian cooking every day, a closed or semi-open layout will save a great deal of cleaning in the long run. For most Delhi NCR homes we work on, a semi-open kitchen with a glass partition or sliding door is the best balance.

Frequently asked questions

Is an open kitchen good for Indian cooking?

An open kitchen can work for Indian cooking if you install a powerful chimney (around 1200 m³/hr suction or more) and ensure good cross-ventilation. For daily heavy frying and tadka, a closed or semi-open kitchen usually keeps the home cleaner.

What is a semi-open kitchen?

A semi-open kitchen is partially separated from the living area by a glass partition, sliding door, half-wall or breakfast counter. It keeps visual connection and light while containing smoke and smells.

Which is better for a small apartment: open or closed kitchen?

Open kitchens make small apartments feel larger and brighter, but closed kitchens offer more storage and contain smells. A semi-open layout with a glass partition is often the best compromise for compact homes.

Can I convert my closed kitchen into an open kitchen?

Often, yes, but first confirm the wall is not structural and check society or builder rules. Plan electrical, plumbing and chimney ducting changes at the same time.
